# Handoff — receiver-visible noncommuting dendrites

**Status:** active dig / falsification program. **Not a novelty claim.**

This is the handoff for the line that grew out of:

> Different morphologies support different modes. Different local states deform those modes. Different receivers see different subsets of them. Computation may consist partly in controlling that deformation.

The sentence survived only after being made much more precise.

---

## 0. What was killed / corrected immediately

### A. “State changes the modes” is not new

Quasi-active dendrite theory and modern reduction work already linearize voltage-dependent dendrites at different operating/holding potentials. The resulting resistance/impedance matrices change with operating point. Wybo et al. explicitly fit quasi-active resistance matrices at multiple holding potentials.

So merely showing

```text
A(x0) != A(x1)
```

or that eigenvalues/eigenvectors move with voltage/channel state is **prior art / expected physics**, not a V23 result.

Reference:
- Wybo et al. (2021), *Data-driven reduction of dendritic morphologies with preserved dendro-somatic responses*, eLife 10:e60936. DOI: https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.60936

### B. “Sequence order matters in dendrites” is not new

Electrical sequence discrimination is classical experimentally, and slower biochemical sequence recognition has also been modeled.

References:
- Branco, Clark & Häusser (2010), *Dendritic discrimination of temporal input sequences in cortical neurons*, Science 329:1671–1675. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1189664
- Bhalla (2017), *Synaptic input sequence discrimination on behavioral timescales mediated by reaction-diffusion chemistry in dendrites*, eLife 6:e25827. DOI: https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.25827

### C. “Lie brackets / commutators in neural dynamical systems” is not new

Maass, Joshi & Sontag explicitly use Lie-bracket/control-theory language for neural circuits.

Reference:
- Maass, Joshi & Sontag (2007), *Computational aspects of feedback in neural circuits*, PLoS Comput Biol 3:e165. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.0020165

### D. Important mathematical correction to the previous chat

It is too casual to take two state-conditioned matrices `A` and `B` and immediately interpret

```text
exp(B dt) exp(A dt) - exp(A dt) exp(B dt)
```

as *the* biological sequence mechanism.

A detailed neuron is a nonlinear dynamical system. The operator is generated by the trajectory itself. If synaptic/current inputs enter additively, the direct input vector fields may even commute, while order dependence can arise through the drift and nested brackets.

Therefore the first principled object should be the **time-ordered local Jacobian along the actual nonlinear trajectory**, not an invented two-state switch.

## 1. The respectable mathematical object: chronological operator excess

Write the detailed neuron as

```text
xdot = F(x,u)
y    = h_R(x)
```

and along one actual stimulus trajectory `x(t)` form the local variational dynamics

```text
d(delta x)/dt = A(t) delta x
A(t) = dF/dx | x(t),u(t)
```

The perturbation propagator is

```text
Phi(T,0) = Texp integral_0^T A(t) dt
```

where `Texp` is the time-ordered exponential.

If all of the instantaneous operators commute,

```text
[A(t1), A(t2)] = 0  for all t1,t2,
```

then time ordering is irrelevant and

```text
Phi(T,0) = exp( integral A(t) dt ).
```

The Magnus expansion makes the first correction explicit:

```text
Omega_1 = integral A(t1) dt1

Omega_2 = 1/2 integral_0^T dt1 integral_0^t1 dt2
              [ A(t1), A(t2) ]
```

with higher nested commutators thereafter.

Reference:
- Magnus (1954), *On the exponential solution of differential equations for a linear operator*, Commun. Pure Appl. Math. 7:649–673. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/cpa.3160070404

This gives a much cleaner V23 question:

> **How much of a dendrite's receiver-visible incremental dynamics is caused by chronological noncommutation of the state-dependent operator, rather than by the time-average of that operator?**

## 2. Receiver-visible metric

Let `P` restrict perturbations to biologically reachable/source directions and let `C_R` read a chosen receiver.

Define the exact local variational propagator

```text
Phi = Texp integral A(t) dt
```

and the order-erased first-Magnus surrogate

```text
Phi_avg = exp( integral A(t) dt ).
```

Prototype receiver-visible chronological excess:

```text
eta_R = || C_R (Phi - Phi_avg) P ||_F
        --------------------------------
             || C_R Phi P ||_F + eps
```

Interpretation:

```text
eta_R ~ 0
    the chronological ordering of local operators contributes little
    to perturbation transfer at receiver R

eta_R large
    preserving only the integrated/average operator is insufficient;
    temporal ordering of state-conditioned transformations matters
```

This is **not** intrinsic complexity. It is a receiver-relative diagnostic.

Also report the second-Magnus approximation

```text
eta_R^(2) based on Omega_2
```

to test whether pairwise commutators are enough or whether higher-order chronology is needed.

## 3. Why Park et al. 2025 is the best calibration organism

Park et al. provide both experiment and a morphologically realistic CA1 model for a clean history-dependent propagation phenomenon.

Experimental/model result:

```text
initial bAP failures
    -> dendritic depolarization
    -> A-type Kv inactivation opens a distal dSpike window
    -> dendritic NaV supports dSpikes
    -> slow NaV inactivation closes the window
```

They report that distal dendrites need roughly 15 ms of prior depolarization before a bAP can trigger a dSpike under the relevant protocol, and the full transient dSpike window lasts roughly 100–180 ms depending on drive.

Their detailed model adds a slow NaV inactivation gate and spatially distributed A-type Kv / NaV properties to a CA1 morphology and reproduces the failure-success-success-failure motif plus period doubling.

Crucially, the numerical simulation code is provided with the paper as Supplementary Software 1.

Reference:
- Park et al. (2025), *Dendritic excitations govern back-propagation via a spike-rate accelerometer*, Nature Communications 16:1333. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-55819-9

Historical anchors showing this is not a newly discovered phenomenon:
- Spruston et al. (1995), *Activity-dependent action potential invasion and calcium influx into hippocampal CA1 dendrites*, Science 268:297–300. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.7716524
- Golding & Spruston (1998), *Dendritic sodium spikes are variable triggers of axonal action potentials in hippocampal CA1 pyramidal neurons*, Neuron 21:1189–1200. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0896-6273(00)80635-3
- Migliore et al. (2005/2007 lineage), CA1 active propagation / morphology and A-current work; see ModelDB lineage used by Park.

V23 is **not** trying to rediscover the accelerometer. It uses it as a known-answer test of the metric.

## 4. Park calibration experiment

### P0 — reproduce the known phenotype

Using their released detailed NEURON model:

```text
soma/proximal step stimulus
record soma + distal apical voltage
reproduce failure -> dSpike -> dSpike -> failure motif
```

Do not proceed if this fails.

### P1 — record state

At fine time resolution save, minimally:

```text
V per compartment
A-type Kv activation/inactivation state
NaV fast activation/inactivation state
NaV slow-inactivation state
```

plus any state needed to reconstruct the exact model RHS.

### P2 — obtain local Jacobian A(t)

Preferred routes, in order:

1. analytic/automatic differentiation if practical;
2. NEURON state perturbation / finite differences on the full RHS;
3. reduced state restricted to voltage + the two identified history gates if full Jacobian is prohibitive.

The result must be checked by predicting short perturbation evolution around saved snapshots.

### P3 — calculate chronology

For windows spanning:

```text
pre-window failure
opening of dSpike window
successful dSpike regime
closing of window
post-window failure
```

compute

```text
Phi
Phi_avg
eta_R
Omega_2 / pairwise commutator contribution
```

for at least:

```text
R = distal dendrite
R = soma
```

Prediction to try to kill:

```text
eta_distal rises around opening/closing of the dSpike window
and falls when the relevant history gates are frozen.
```

No prediction is yet earned for `eta_soma`; Park's pure optogenetic phenomenon is primarily a dendritic propagation phenotype, and the soma can be comparatively blind to distal dSpike identity.

### P4 — biological ablations

Keep stimulus and morphology fixed.

```text
freeze A-type Kv inactivation state
freeze slow NaV inactivation state
remove spatial A-type Kv gradient but preserve total/max conductance budget
remove spatial slow-NaV-inactivation gradient but preserve budget
```

Read out both phenotype and `eta_R`.

The metric is useful only if it tracks the known causal mechanism rather than merely total excitation.

### P5 — chronology null

For the same recorded `A(t)` trajectory compare:

```text
full time-ordered Phi
vs
exp(integral A dt)
```

This is the cleanest mathematical order-erasure control.

A secondary diagnostic can shuffle short `A(t)` blocks while preserving their multiset. This is **not** a biological intervention; it is only a systems diagnostic of chronological sensitivity.

## 5. Modal analysis is secondary, not primary

For selected snapshots, decompose

```text
A(t) v_n(t) = lambda_n(t) v_n(t)
```

and, where numerically meaningful, track:

```text
pole drift
mode rotation
receiver/source residue
```

A mode's receiver importance depends on excitation and observation, not just on its eigenvalue.

For a locally linear system, schematically:

```text
R_n ~ (C_R v_n) (w_n^* B)
```

A strong biological effect may therefore be caused by **modal accessibility/residue changes with little eigenvalue movement**.

This is a direct possibility in Park: the distal propagation pathway can open and close while the global neuron has not acquired an entirely new set of physical degrees of freedom.

The modal result is explanatory only if it agrees with the trajectory-level chronology test.

## 6. Volterra bridge — independent measurement route

Nonlinear neural input/output transformations have a long Volterra-system-identification history.

Recent particularly relevant result:
- Gattas et al. (2026), *A concise mathematical description of signal transformations across the hippocampal apical CA3 to CA1 dendritic response*, reports >94% description of apical fEPSP dynamics with a second-order Volterra equation, with basal dendrites showing evidence for higher required order. PubMed PMID 41768319.

Older methodological anchors:
- Marmarelis & Berger (2005), *General methodology for nonlinear modeling of neural systems with Poisson point-process inputs*, Math Biosci. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mbs.2005.04.002
- Korenberg & Hunter (1996), *The identification of nonlinear biological systems: Volterra kernel approaches*, Ann Biomed Eng.

This suggests an independent test:

```text
input A at dendritic source i
input B at dendritic source j
measure receiver R
estimate first- and second-order cross-kernels
```

The experimentally accessible target is **order-antisymmetric cross-interaction** after subtracting first-order fixed-kernel effects.

Do not call an ordinary second-order Volterra term a commutator. The useful comparison is whether the measured order-specific nonlinear interaction covaries with `eta_R` / the Magnus commutator contribution in the mechanistic model.

If it does, we have a bridge from full-state mechanism to input/output measurement.

## 7. Critical control-theory distinction

For a control-affine nonlinear system

```text
xdot = f(x) + sum_i u_i g_i(x)
```

Lie brackets such as

```text
[g_i, g_j]
[f, g_i]
[f, [f, g_i]]
...
```

characterize locally generated directions / noncommuting flows.

But for simple additive current injection, `g_i` can be constant, so

```text
[g_i, g_j] = 0
```

while the neuron still has order dependence because the drift `f` evolves voltage and channel state between events.

Therefore V23 must **not** equate:

```text
AB-vs-BA response difference
```

with

```text
[g_A, g_B]
```

without deriving the specific augmented-state model.

Maass et al. (2007) is the prior-art guardrail here.

## 8. Where TwinProp enters, only after Park calibration

TwinProp already reports:

```text
higher parity dimensionality
    -> higher-rank dendritic voltage PCA
    -> stronger / more widespread NMDA recruitment

and causal performance losses for:
    passive dendrites
    soma-only morphology
    no-NMDA
```

Reference:
- Aizenbud et al. (2026), *What can a neuron compute*, bioRxiv 2026.06.08.730984. DOI: https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.06.08.730984

V23 must not rename their voltage PCA result.

The new test would be:

```text
Does receiver-visible chronological excess eta_R
rise with task dimensionality
AND add predictive information beyond:
    mean NMDA current
    dendritic-voltage PCA rank
    voltage variance
    number of recruited compartments
    spike count / firing rate
?
```

If no: kill the bridge.

If yes only locally but not at soma/axon: local dendritic mechanism, not yet a single-neuron I/O complexity explanation.


Each arrow needs its own intervention.

## 11. Current research sentence

> **The candidate is no longer “state changes the neuron's modes.” The sharper hypothesis is that local history makes the neuron's incremental operator vary in time, and morphology determines whether the resulting noncommuting chronological transformations remain distinguishable at a chosen receiver.**

Equivalent operational question:

> **How much computation disappears when the same sequence of instantaneous local linear operators is replaced by its order-erased exponential average?**

That is the next thing to measure.
